Editing GTFS feeds

GTFS feeds describe the transit services of a city or region as they are. Quite often, however, one wants to analyze how things would be if they were different: what if a bus route ran more frequently, or if a rail line were faster? Answering such questions requires editing the feed, changing its service levels, travel times or the way its schedules are described, and then feeding the result to routing and accessibility tools.

gtfstools includes several functions to edit GTFS feeds. The editing functions currently available are:

  • set_route_frequency(): sets the headway of routes within a time of day;
  • set_trip_speed(): sets the average speed of trips, or of a segment of them;
  • stop_times_to_frequencies(): approximates scheduled trips by frequency-based ones;
  • interpolate_stop_times(): fills blank stop times, assuming a constant speed between stops with known times;
  • interpolate_shape_dist_traveled(): calculates the distances, in meters, travelled along the shapes up to each stop and shape point, replacing any existing ones;
  • set_route_speed(): sets the average speed of the trips of routes, or of a segment of them;
  • set_max_speed(): caps the speed of trips, slowing down only the parts that are faster than a maximum speed;
  • set_dwell_time(): sets how long vehicles stay at stops;
  • frequencies_to_stop_times(): converts frequency-based trips into scheduled ones;
  • merge_gtfs(): combines several feeds into a single one;
  • remove_duplicates(): removes duplicated entries from the feed’s tables;
  • remove_unused_ids(): removes the ids that the feed doesn’t use, with their entries in every table;
  • fix_gtfs(): cleans a feed in one call, removing duplicated entries and unused ids, optionally capping the speed of trips, filling blank stop times, optionally setting a minimum dwell time at intermediate stops and recalculating the distances travelled along shapes (replacing any existing ones).

This vignette will introduce you to the first four functions and to fix_gtfs(), and will cover their usage in detail. By default, they return an edited copy of the feed and leave the given GTFS object unchanged.

We will start by loading the packages required by this demonstration into the current R session:

library(gtfstools)
library(dplyr)

Setting route frequencies

The number of departures of a route is one of the main levers of a transit network, and one that is frequently changed in scenario analyses. get_route_frequency() tells us how many departures each route has within a time of day, and its mean headway. Let’s see how frequently the route T2 of Porto Alegre’s feed runs during the morning peak:

path <- system.file("extdata/poa_gtfs.zip", package = "gtfstools")
gtfs <- read_gtfs(path)

get_route_frequency(
  gtfs,
  route_id = "T2",
  from = "07:00:00",
  to = "09:00:00"
)
#> Key: <route_id, direction_id, service_id>
#>    route_id direction_id service_id departures mean_headway
#>      <char>        <int>     <char>      <int>        <num>
#> 1:       T2            0       T2@1         17     7.058824
#> 2:       T2            0       T2@2          8    15.000000
#> 3:       T2            0       T2@5          5    24.000000

The route runs on three different services (T2@1 on weekdays, for example), each with a different headway. set_route_frequency() sets a single headway, in minutes, to all of them. Let’s say we want the route to run every 10 minutes from 7 to 9 am:

new_gtfs <- set_route_frequency(
  gtfs,
  route_id = "T2",
  headway = 10,
  from = "07:00:00",
  to = "09:00:00"
)

get_route_frequency(
  new_gtfs,
  route_id = "T2",
  from = "07:00:00",
  to = "09:00:00"
)
#> Key: <route_id, direction_id, service_id>
#>    route_id direction_id service_id departures mean_headway
#>      <char>        <int>     <char>      <int>        <num>
#> 1:       T2            0       T2@1         12           10
#> 2:       T2            0       T2@2         12           10
#> 3:       T2            0       T2@5         12           10

new_gtfs$frequencies
#> Index: <trip_id>
#>       trip_id start_time end_time headway_secs exact_times
#>        <char>     <char>   <char>        <int>       <int>
#> 1: T2-1@1#702   07:00:00 09:00:00          600           0
#> 2: T2-1@2#701   07:00:00 09:00:00          600           0
#> 3: T2-1@5#701   07:00:00 09:00:00          600           0

Under the hood, the function replaces the trips that depart within the time of day by a single template trip per service (and per direction, when direction_id is present in trips), taken from its most common sequence of stops. The departures of the template are described in the frequencies table. Since this feed had no frequencies table, one has been created. The template keeps its travel times between stops, which apply to all of the new departures. Departures outside the time of day are kept as before. Please note that departures after midnight are listed with times greater than "24:00:00", so a time of day such as "24:00:00"-"26:00:00" should be used for them.

The trips removed by the function may leave unused entries in other tables, such as shapes and calendar, which can be removed with remove_unused_ids(). If your routing software doesn’t support frequency-based trips, frequencies_to_stop_times() converts them back into scheduled trips.

Setting trip speeds

set_trip_speed() sets the average speed of trips by changing the arrival_time and departure_time columns in the stop_times table. Let’s see how that works using the trip CPTM L07-0 of São Paulo’s feed. This trip is described in the frequencies table, so its times are just a “template” that describes how long it takes from one stop to another, relative to each of its departures.

path <- system.file("extdata/spo_gtfs.zip", package = "gtfstools")
spo_gtfs <- read_gtfs(path)

get_trip_speed(spo_gtfs, trip_id = "CPTM L07-0")
#> Key: <trip_id>
#>    route_id    trip_id    speed
#>      <char>     <char>    <num>
#> 1: CPTM L07 CPTM L07-0 26.73002

# 'unit' argument defaults to "km/h"
new_speed_gtfs <- set_trip_speed(spo_gtfs, trip_id = "CPTM L07-0", speed = 50)

get_trip_speed(new_speed_gtfs, trip_id = "CPTM L07-0")
#> Key: <trip_id>
#>    route_id    trip_id    speed
#>      <char>     <char>    <num>
#> 1: CPTM L07 CPTM L07-0 50.00388

new_speed_gtfs$stop_times |>
  filter(trip_id == "CPTM L07-0") |>
  select(stop_id, stop_sequence, arrival_time, departure_time)
#> Index: <trip_id>
#>     stop_id stop_sequence arrival_time departure_time
#>      <char>         <int>       <char>         <char>
#>  1:   18940             1     04:00:00       04:00:00
#>  2:   18920             2                            
#>  3:   18919             3                            
#>  4:   18917             4                            
#>  5:   18916             5                            
#>  6:   18965             6                            
#>  7:   18923             7                            
#>  8:   18922             8                            
#>  9: 4114459             9                            
#> 10:   18921            10                            
#> 11:   18924            11                            
#> 12:   18925            12                            
#> 13:   18926            13                            
#> 14:   18971            14                            
#> 15:   18972            15                            
#> 16:   18973            16                            
#> 17:   18974            17                            
#> 18:   18975            18     05:12:42       05:12:42

The duration of the trip is set to its length, measured along its shape, divided by the given speed. set_trip_speed() updates the arrival time at the last stop accordingly and sets the arrival and departure times at intermediate stops to "". To fill in the expected arrival and departure times of intermediate stops, you use interpolate_stop_times(), as we’ll see in a later section.

The set_trip_speed() function also includes a few other arguments that control which trips and parts of trips are changed:

  • unit sets the unit in which the speed is given, either "km/h" (the default), "m/s" or "mph";
  • first_stop and last_stop set the speed of only a segment of the trips, between two stops. The times at the stops after the segment are shifted by the change in its duration;
  • from and to only change the trips that depart from the segment’s first stop between from “HH:MM:SS” and to “HH:MM:SS” (both included);
  • by_reference = TRUE updates the stop_times table by reference, thus changing the given GTFS object.

Converting stop times to frequencies

Some routing engines and other tools treat frequency-based trips differently from scheduled ones. For example, the time_window parameter of {r5r} draws departure times within the headways of frequency-based trips. stop_times_to_frequencies() converts scheduled trips, described only in stop_times, into frequency-based trips, described in frequencies. Let’s convert all the trips of Porto Alegre’s feed:

converted_gtfs <- stop_times_to_frequencies(gtfs)

nrow(gtfs$trips)
#> [1] 387
nrow(converted_gtfs$trips)
#> [1] 157

# frequencies of route T2 on weekdays, from 6 to 10 am
converted_gtfs$frequencies |>
  filter(
    startsWith(trip_id, "T2-1@1#"),
    start_time >= "06:00:00",
    start_time < "10:00:00"
  )
#>       trip_id start_time end_time headway_secs exact_times
#>        <char>     <char>   <char>        <int>       <int>
#> 1: T2-1@1#610   06:00:00 07:00:00          515           0
#> 2: T2-1@1#702   07:00:00 08:00:00          400           0
#> 3: T2-1@1#800   08:00:00 09:00:00          450           0
#> 4: T2-1@1#910   09:00:00 10:00:00          900           0

# the number of departures of route T2 is kept
get_route_frequency(
  converted_gtfs,
  route_id = "T2",
  from = "07:00:00",
  to = "09:00:00"
)
#> Key: <route_id, direction_id, service_id>
#>    route_id direction_id service_id departures mean_headway
#>      <char>        <int>     <char>      <int>        <num>
#> 1:       T2            0       T2@1         17     7.058824
#> 2:       T2            0       T2@2          8    15.000000
#> 3:       T2            0       T2@5          5    24.000000

The trips that share the same route, service, direction, shape and sequence of stops are grouped together and split into one-hour slots of the clock, according to their earliest departure time. In each slot, the trip that departs first becomes a template trip, and a frequencies entry is added for it, from the start to the end of the slot, with a headway of ceiling(3600 / n) seconds, n being the number of trips in the slot (e.g. the 9 trips of route T2 that depart between 7 and 8 am on weekdays give a headway of 400 seconds). The other trip ids in the same template are removed from the trips.txt file, but it keeps the same number of departures.

Please note that this is a lossy approximation of the original schedule: the exact departure times of the removed trips, their travel times and other fields in trips (e.g. trip_headsign) are lost. Converting the result back with frequencies_to_stop_times() yields the same number of trips in each slot (as long as it has at most 60 trips), but their departures are evenly spaced from the start of the slot. To convert only some trips, pass their ids to the trip_id argument.

Interpolating stop times

Sometimes, GTFS feeds don’t report the arrival and departure times at every stop: only the times at the first and last stops of each trip are required, and the stops in between may be left blank. The stops with known times are called timepoints. In Porto Alegre’s feed, for example, only the first and last stops of each trip have times:

trip <- "T2-1@1#520"

gtfs$stop_times |> 
  filter(trip_id == trip) |> 
  select(stop_id, arrival_time, departure_time) |> 
  head()
#>    stop_id arrival_time departure_time
#>     <char>       <char>         <char>
#> 1:    3609     05:20:00       05:20:00
#> 2:    3608                            
#> 3:    3564                            
#> 4:    6336                            
#> 5:    3633                            
#> 6:    5544

interpolate_stop_times() fills these blank times, assuming that the vehicle travels between two consecutive timepoints at a constant speed, with distances measured along the trip’s shape:

interpolated_gtfs <- interpolate_stop_times(gtfs, trip_id = trip)

interpolated_gtfs$stop_times |> 
  filter(trip_id == trip) |> 
  select(stop_id, arrival_time, departure_time) |> 
  head()
#>    stop_id arrival_time departure_time
#>     <char>       <char>         <char>
#> 1:    3609     05:20:00       05:20:00
#> 2:    3608     05:20:27       05:20:27
#> 3:    3564     05:22:00       05:22:00
#> 4:    6336     05:24:02       05:24:02
#> 5:    3633     05:25:55       05:25:55
#> 6:    5544     05:26:31       05:26:31

Interpolated stops get equal arrival and departure times (i.e. no dwell time), rounded to the nearest second, and timepoint = 0 when the stop_times table includes a timepoint column. In feeds without shapes, use method = "euclidean" to measure distances as straight lines between stops instead.

If trip_id is not given, the function interpolates the data to give explicit stop times at every stop of every trip in the feed. Some stops may be left blank, with a warning: for example, a few trips of Porto Alegre’s feed list their times after midnight as "00:xx:xx", instead of "24:xx:xx", so they seem to arrive before they depart and can’t be interpolated.

Fixing common problems

GTFS feeds often come with small problems that get in the way of an analysis, such as duplicated entries, ids that no trip uses, blank stop times and missing distances travelled along shapes. fix_gtfs() fixes them in one call, chaining several of the functions listed above.

In this example below, we fix Porto Alegre’s feed, capping maximum speeds to 25 Km/h and making vehicles stay at least 20 seconds at each intermediate stop for passengers to board and alight:

fixed_gtfs <- fix_gtfs(
  gtfs, 
  max_speed = 25, 
  min_dwell_time = 20
  )
#> Warning: 10 trips have stops whose arrival is earlier than the previous departure, so
#> their speeds were not capped between them: "176-1@1#2310", "A141-1@3#2340",
#> "A141-1@5#2340", "T2-1@1#2310", "T2-1@1#2332" and 5 more.
#> ℹ This usually happens when times after midnight are written as "00:30:00"
#>   instead of "24:30:00".
#> Warning: 558 stop times could not be interpolated and were left blank.
#> ℹ 10 trips have an arrival earlier than the previous departure, e.g. times
#>   after midnight written as "00:30:00" instead of "24:30:00": "176-1@1#2310",
#>   "A141-1@3#2340", "A141-1@5#2340", "T2-1@1#2310", "T2-1@1#2332",
#>   "T2-1@1#2357", "T2-1@2#2332", "T2-1@2#2357", "T2-1@5#2334", and
#>   "T2-1@5#2357".

With fix_gtfs(), the feed goes through the following steps, in this order:

  1. remove_duplicates() removes duplicated entries from every table;
  2. remove_unused_ids() removes the trips without stop times and the entries of every table that no remaining trip uses (e.g. stops that are not used in any trip);
  3. set_max_speed(), only if max_speed is given, slows down the parts of trips that are faster than max_speed;
  4. interpolate_stop_times() fills blank stop times. Since this runs after the speed cap, the filled times follow the capped ones;
  5. set_min_dwell_time() forces every vehicle to remain stopped at each intermediate stop for at least min_dwell_time for passengers to board and alight;
  6. interpolate_shape_dist_traveled(), in feeds with shapes, adds the distances, in meters, travelled from the start of each shape to each stop and shape point.

Note:

All editing functions return a GTFS object readily available to be manipulated and analyzed using the rest of gtfstools’ toolkit, or to be written to disk with write_gtfs(). For more information on how to use other functions made available by the package, please see the introductory vignette and the function reference.
